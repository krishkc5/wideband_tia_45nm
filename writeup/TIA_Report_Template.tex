\section{Process Characterization (Part A)}

All simulations use the 45\,nm CMOS process with minimum channel length
$L=45\,\mathrm{nm}$. Device widths $W=\{0.5, 1, 2, 4, 8\}\,\mu\mathrm{m}$
are swept. NMOS devices are characterized with $V_{GS}=1\,\mathrm{V}$ and PMOS
with $V_{SG}=1\,\mathrm{V}$. Each metric is presented with:
\begin{enumerate}[noitemsep]
    \item analytical expectations,
    \item simulation setup,
    \item NMOS results,
    \item PMOS results,
    \item combined discussion.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{$V_{TH}$--$I_D$ Extraction}

\subsubsection*{Analytical Background}
For a diode-connected MOSFET in saturation,
\[
I_D \approx \frac{1}{2}\mu C_{\mathrm{ox}}\frac{W}{L}(V_{GS}-V_{TH})^2.
\]
Thus, solving for $V_{TH}$ gives
\[
V_{TH} \approx V_{GS} - \sqrt{\frac{2L I_D}{\mu C_{\mathrm{ox}} W}}.
\]
In practice, the simulator provides an operating-point value for $v_{th}$ that
tracks this relationship.

\subsubsection*{Simulation Setup}
A diode-connected device is biased with a swept DC current source. For each bias
point, the operating-point threshold voltage is saved.

\subsubsection*{NMOS Results}
\textit{Figure placeholder: NMOS $V_{TH}$ vs $I_D$ plot.}

NMOS threshold voltage remains approximately constant between
\SI{0.60}{V} and \SI{0.62}{V} across several decades of current.

\subsubsection*{PMOS Results}
\textit{Figure placeholder: PMOS $V_{TH}$ vs $I_{SD}$ plot.}

PMOS threshold voltage lies near \SI{0.52}{V}–\SI{0.54}{V}, with mild width

\subsubsection*{Discussion}
Both device polarities show weak variation of $V_{TH}$ with $I_D$, confirming
that threshold voltage may be treated as approximately constant in early hand
calculations.

\subsection{$g_m$--$I_D$ Characteristics}

\subsubsection*{Analytical Background}
The transconductance is
\[
g_m = \frac{\partial I_D}{\partial V_{GS}}
= \mu C_{\mathrm{ox}}\frac{W}{L}(V_{GS}-V_{TH})
\]
in long-channel strong inversion, with $g_m$ increasing monotonically with $I_D$.
Short-channel behavior exhibits compression at high fields.

\subsubsection*{Simulation Setup}
The same diode-connected testbench is used. The simulator directly reports $g_m$.

\subsection{Gate Capacitances $C_{gs}$ and $C_{gd}$}

\subsubsection*{Analytical Background}
At minimum length, input capacitances are dominated by overlap and fringing:
\[
C_{gs} \approx C_{\mathrm{ov}}W + C_{\mathrm{fringe}}, \qquad
C_{gd} \approx C_{\mathrm{ov}}W + C_{\mathrm{fringe}}.
\]
Thus, both scale approximately linearly with $W$.

\subsubsection*{Simulation Setup}
With the device in a diode-connected configuration and a small AC voltage
applied, capacitances are obtained from AC operating-point data.

\subsection{Unity-Gain Frequency $f_T$}

\subsubsection*{Analytical Background}
The unity-gain frequency is
\[
f_T = \frac{g_m}{2\pi(C_{gs}+C_{gd})}.
\]
Higher $g_m$ or lower capacitances increase $f_T$. Short-channel devices exhibit
saturation of $f_T$ at high current.

\subsubsection*{Simulation Setup}
A small-signal AC simulation is run with the drain current gain measured. $f_T$ is
the frequency where $|i_d/i_g|=1$.
